Method, data and validation

How every number on this site is made

Everything here can be rebuilt from our open code and public data. Nothing is trained: the scores come from a fixed formula with weights we set before seeing any results.

What a score means

A score ranks Earth places against each other for one mission and one Mars site. It is not a percentage of similarity to Mars. A 93 does not mean "93% Mars". It means the place is close to the best Earth match on the features that mission cares about. The distance that remains is the Gap Index.

s_j(e,t) = 1 - |x_ej - x_tj| / max over Earth sites e' of |x_e'j - x_tj| score(e,t) = 100 * sum_j w_mj * s_j(e,t) / sum_j w_mj gap_j(e,t) = |x_ej - x_tj| / (max - min of feature j over all Earth and Mars places) Gap(e,t) = 100 * mean_j gap_j(e,t)

x is a feature value, e an Earth place, t a Mars site, j a feature and w the mission's weight. The score is relative (Earth places compared with each other); the Gap Index is absolute (0 means identical to Mars, 100 means as far apart as anything we measured). The score range on each brief comes from recomputing the score 500 times with weights drawn around the chosen ones (a Dirichlet distribution) and keeping the 5th to 95th percentile.

Validation

The method is never told which places are known analogs. We check afterwards whether the 11 known analogs score above the 5 negative controls. AUC is the share of the 55 pairs ranked correctly: 1.00 is perfect, 0.50 a coin flip. Equal weights give 0.74; mission weights give 0.89 on average.

Known analogs: Atacama (Yungay), Haughton Crater, Devon Is., Death Valley, McMurdo Dry Valleys (Wright), MDRS, Utah, Rio Tinto (Peña de Hierro), Bockfjorden, Svalbard, Lanzarote, Pilbara, Australia, Holuhraun, Iceland, Mauna Kea, Hawaii.
Negative controls: Amazon rainforest, Flevoland farmland, Congo rainforest, Black Forest, Germany, Sundarbans, chosen because they are wet, green or farmed, the opposite of Mars.

What this does and does not show: nothing is trained, so the test sites cannot leak into a model. But the controls are deliberately clear-cut, so a high AUC shows the method tells Mars-like from non-Mars-like places. It does not prove the fine order among similar deserts. A harder test, with deserts that experts do not use as analogs, is our next step.

Limits

  • Earth cannot reproduce Mars's gravity, thin air, radiation or magnetic field; every brief lists these gaps.
  • Pixels are 463 m to 4 km and each place is a 20 x 20 km box; small features inside a box are averaged away.
  • Mars temperatures come from a climate model, ground ice from published maps, rain from a non-agency dataset.
  • No geology, minerals, dust or caves yet. Moon sites are not included yet.
