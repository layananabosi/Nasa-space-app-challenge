Regional search, 444 squares of 20 by 20 km

What if the best place to practice is not on anyone's list yet?

We split the Negev, Sinai, southern Jordan and the edges of Egypt and Saudi Arabia into a grid and measured every square with exactly the same NASA and ESA data and the same mission rules as our 23 sites. Squares more than 30 km from every site we started with are marked "new to our list". Some may already be known to geologists; they are places worth checking.

Use the layer menu on the map to switch missions and Mars sites. Ice drilling is left out: there is no ground ice in this region.

Best squares for each mission

Read this before the numbers: a square is an average over 400 square kilometres, which can hold roads, towns and very different rock. A high score, even 100, marks an area worth a closer look on the ground. It is not proof that a place is suitable or safe for a real mission test.

Scores use the same 0 to 100 scale as the briefs, so a square scoring above the best of our 23 sites is a better match than any place we started with.

How to read this

A square is an average over 400 square kilometres, so a high score points to an area worth a closer look, not a finished answer. Cities, farms and roads inside a square lower its bare-ground value.
